1 · The macro crisis (SDG 2 & SDG 13)

A silent harvest thief

Plants provide around 80% of the food humans eat. Yet the Food and Agriculture Organization estimates that 20–40% of global crop production is lost to pests and diseases every year, with plant diseases alone costing the world economy more than US$220 billion annually [1]. A global survey of plant-health experts found average losses of 21.5% for wheat, 30% for rice, 22.5% for maize and 17.2% for potato — with the highest losses in food-deficit regions with fast-growing populations [2].

Climate change is a catalyst. Warmer temperatures and erratic rainfall extend the seasons in which pathogens can reproduce and open new territory: an analysis of 612 crop pests and pathogens showed their ranges have shifted poleward by roughly 3 km per year since 1960 [3]. Diseases once confined to humid tropical farmland increasingly appear in dense urban gardens, rooftops and school plots — exactly where there is no agronomist.

2 · Why conventional solutions fail

The chemical trap & the expert gap

Guess-spraying. When a home or community plant gets sick, the reflex is a broad-spectrum chemical spray — often the wrong one (fungicides do nothing against bacteria or viruses, and yellow leaves are frequently just a nutrient deficiency). Over-use drives pathogen resistance, harms soil organisms and pollinators, and leaches into groundwater. The world already applies about 3.5 million tonnes of pesticide active ingredients per year [4].

No access to diagnosis. Laboratory phytopathology is slow and costly. By the time a result arrives — days or weeks later — a fast pathogen such as late blight can destroy a whole plot in 7–10 days and has already spread to neighbours.

3 · Our thesis

Every smartphone, a plant-quarantine agent

Agralytic AI inverts the paradigm from reactive chemical treatment to proactive biological defence, following the Integrated Pest Management hierarchy promoted by FAO [5]: prevent → monitor → isolate → least-toxic treatment.

Detect in seconds — on-device.

A computer-vision pipeline runs inside the browser (edge AI). No upload, no data cost, works offline, protects privacy.

Treat with what’s in the kitchen.

Evidence-ranked organic recipes with phytotoxicity limits, a dose calculator and a guided checklist.

Isolate before it spreads.

Anonymous reports feed a geofenced radar. More than 3 same-pathogen cases within 1 km trigger automatic SMS/WhatsApp alerts with wind-aware spore-drift warnings.

Educate a generation.

3D anatomy, microscopic simulations, quizzes and an open Diagnostics Lab turn users into informed Bio-Agents.

4 · Methodology & limitations

How we keep it honest

  • Vision model: HSV colour segmentation + morphological leaf extraction + 18 interpretable lesion features → FeatureNet (MLP 18-24-11) trained in-browser on augmented procedural leaves. Its test accuracy is reported live in the Diagnostics Lab. Synthetic accuracy is an upper bound; real-world accuracy must be measured in the field (see validation below).
  • Deep-learning upgrade path: a MobileNetV2 transfer-learning script for the public PlantVillage dataset [6] is included; the exported TensorFlow.js model plugs in automatically.
  • Treatment safety: every concentration is capped below published phytotoxicity levels; sources are listed on each recipe.
  • Privacy by design: on-device inference, EXIF/GPS stripping, face blurring, ~100 m location rounding, AES-256-GCM encryption of alert numbers, no third-party trackers.
  • Not a replacement for professional diagnosis in severe cases — the app escalates to extension officers.
